      This is an illustration, not financial advice. I'm not a regulated financial adviser. Projections use simplified, steady-return assumptions in today's money; markets are volatile and returns aren't guaranteed. Take-home uses 2025/26 England income-tax bands (with personal-allowance taper) and employee NI at the margin; salary sacrifice assumes full relief; the £60k annual allowance is applied and tapered above ~£260k adjusted income to a £10k floor (a simplified version of the real rules); Scotland differs. Housing assumes ~2.5% inflation to convert the fixed-cash mortgage to today's money, a repayment mortgage amortising over the remaining term, and your chosen real house-price growth. Both partners assumed to build full State Pension entitlement. Outside-pension savings are split into an ISA (£20k/person a year, tax-free) and a taxable GIA for the overflow; your existing pot is treated as ISA. The drawdown holds your gross income constant at the safe-withdrawal amount (SWR × pot, in today's money) so the net-worth run-down stays consistent; tax then reduces the net you keep, following a fixed tax-efficient order — pension within the personal allowance, then ISA, then GIA (CGT allowance then a flat 18% on gains), then pension at 20%/40%; pensions are modelled as UFPLS (25% of each withdrawal tax-free, 75% taxable) rather than a single upfront tax-free lump sum, with the tax-free element capped at the £268,275 lump-sum allowance per person (beyond which withdrawals are fully taxable). The State Pension replaces part of the pot withdrawal once it starts (rather than adding on top), keeping gross income level. Real CGT band interactions, the tax-free-lump-sum cap and Scottish rates are simplified. Windfalls are entered as the amount you actually receive in today's money and land in the GIA — UK inheritances aren't taxed as income in the recipient's hands, though the estate may have paid inheritance tax before you receive it. For decisions, consider an FCA-regulated independent financial adviser.
