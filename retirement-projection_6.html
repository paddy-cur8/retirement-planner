How to read this, and the ideas behind it

Salary sacrifice turns a taxed raise into an untaxed contribution. A pound taken as salary in the higher-rate band reaches your investments as roughly 58p; sacrificed into a pension it arrives as a full pound, and you also save employee National Insurance. That's why the pension band grows fast when contributions are high. The model caps pension input at the £60,000 annual allowance and routes anything above it to taken-home pay (taxed, then partly invested) — anyone whose total income approaches ~£260k sees the allowance taper, which isn't modelled here and would pull that ceiling down.

The catch is the lock. Money in a pension is inaccessible until the minimum pension age — currently 55, rising to 57 in 2028 and likely higher for younger savers. So if you plan to stop work before then, your accessible pot (ISAs and taxable investments) has to cover the years until the pension unlocks. The early-retirement bridge tile turns green only when accessible savings actually cover that gap; if it flags a shortfall, the fix is to sacrifice a little less into pensions and let more flow into ISAs.

Home equity builds from two forces. Rather than a flat assumption, equity is modelled as the property value (growing at your chosen real house-price growth) minus a mortgage that amortises to zero over its remaining term. Because mortgage debt is fixed in cash terms, inflation quietly erodes it in real terms too. Equity climbs as the loan is paid down — though it stays illiquid unless the home is sold or downsized.

The levers that move the needle

  • Save a bigger share of pay rises. At a young age, every extra pound invested compounds for decades — the biggest lever most people have.
  • Use pension tax relief, but mind the lock. Salary sacrifice is highly tax-efficient; the "sacrifice" and "pension share" sliders trade that efficiency against early-access flexibility. Keep the bridge tile green.
  • Overpay the mortgage vs invest? If your mortgage rate is close to your expected investment return, paying it down is a guaranteed, risk-free return worth weighing against investing.
  • Stay invested, low-cost, global. The long-run real return is the biggest swing factor and the one you don't control — a cheap, diversified, equity-heavy portfolio is the usual answer.

Projections use simplified, steady-return assumptions in today's money; markets are volatile and returns aren't guaranteed. Take-home uses 2025/26 England income-tax bands (with personal-allowance taper) and employee NI at the margin; salary sacrifice assumes full relief with pension input capped at the £60k annual allowance (taper for very high earners not modelled); Scotland differs. Housing assumes ~2.5% inflation to convert the fixed-cash mortgage to today's money, a repayment mortgage amortising over the remaining term, and your chosen real house-price growth. Both partners assumed to build full State Pension entitlement. Windfalls are entered as the amount you actually receive in today's money and land as accessible cash — UK inheritances aren't taxed as income in the recipient's hands, though the estate may have paid inheritance tax before you receive it.
